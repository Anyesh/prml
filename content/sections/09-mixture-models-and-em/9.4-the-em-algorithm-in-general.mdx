## Intuition

Every earlier claim in this chapter, that EM's log-likelihood never falls, that K-means is
a limit of it, rested on inspecting specific formulas for a Gaussian mixture. None of that
proves anything about a different model with its own unobserved structure: a hidden Markov
model, a mixture of Bernoullis, a Bayesian linear regression with the weights treated as
missing. This section proves the general claim once, for any such model, with a single
picture: a curve, a bound that touches it, and the bound's own peak climbing the curve one
step at a time.

The reason a stand-in bound can be trusted at all is that it is never above the true curve.
Improving the stand-in, which is easy, therefore never overstates how much the real thing
improved. It can only understate it.

## The math

For any distribution $q$ over the unobserved variables $\mathbf{Z}$, the log-likelihood
splits exactly into two pieces:

<MathBlock id="9.70" name="exact decomposition of the log-likelihood">

$$\ln p(\mathbf{X}\mid\boldsymbol\theta) = L(q,\boldsymbol\theta) + \mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,p)$$

</MathBlock>

<MathBlock id="9.71" name="the lower bound functional">

$$L(q,\boldsymbol\theta) = \sum_{\mathbf{Z}} q(\mathbf{Z})\,\ln\frac{p(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}\mid\boldsymbol\theta)}{q(\mathbf{Z})}$$

</MathBlock>

<MathBlock id="9.72" name="the KL divergence term">

$$\mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,p) = -\sum_{\mathbf{Z}} q(\mathbf{Z})\,\ln\frac{p(\mathbf{Z}\mid\mathbf{X},\boldsymbol\theta)}{q(\mathbf{Z})}$$

</MathBlock>

Because a KL divergence is never negative, $L$ never exceeds the true log-likelihood, for
any $q$ at all. The E-step picks $q=p(\mathbf{Z}\mid\mathbf{X},\boldsymbol\theta^{\text{old}})$,
the one choice that zeroes the KL term and makes $L$ touch the curve exactly. Substituting
that choice back in shows $L$ reduces to last section's $Q$ function plus a $q$-only
constant:

<Figure caption="The true log-likelihood curve and the bound built from one E-step, touching at exactly the theta that E-step used." source="9.12">
  <BoundTouchesCurve client:visible />
</Figure>

<MathBlock id="9.74" name="the bound after an E-step equals Q">

$$L(q,\boldsymbol\theta) = Q(\boldsymbol\theta,\boldsymbol\theta^{\text{old}}) + \text{const}$$

</MathBlock>

The M-step then maximises $L$ over $\boldsymbol\theta$ with $q$ held fixed. Because $L$
still cannot exceed the curve, and the curve at the new $\boldsymbol\theta$ is at least as
high as $L$ there, the log-likelihood's increase is at least as large as the bound's.

<Figure caption="The KL gap between the bound and the curve, as a function of theta, for the q fixed at one particular point. Zero exactly where q was computed, positive everywhere else." source="9.11">

</Figure>

## Worked example

One parameter, two fixed-variance Gaussians, one free mean: starting the free mean far from
either cluster gives a log-likelihood of $-26.31$. One EM round moves it to $-21.54$, a
second to $-21.30$, and a third leaves it unchanged, having converged. At the exact E-step
distribution the gap between bound and curve is $3.6\times10^{-15}$, numerically zero; pull
that same distribution 60% of the way towards a uniform split instead and the gap opens to
$12.82$ nats at the same parameters.

## Recall

<Recall question="Why does maximising a lower bound guarantee the true log-likelihood does not decrease?" concepts={["em-lower-bound"]}>
Because the bound never exceeds the true log-likelihood for any q, at the new parameters
the log-likelihood is at least as large as the bound there, and the M-step chose those
parameters specifically to raise the bound above its value at the old parameters, which
equalled the old log-likelihood exactly.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What does the KL gap measure, and when is it exactly zero?" concepts={["kl-gap"]}>
It measures how far q is from the true posterior over the unobserved variables at the
current parameters. It is exactly zero right after an E-step, because the E-step's whole
job is choosing q to equal that posterior, and generally becomes positive again once the
M-step moves the parameters without updating q.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What single fact makes the general EM argument apply to models far from Gaussian mixtures?" concepts={["expectation-maximisation"]}>
The decomposition ln p(X|theta) = L(q,theta) + KL(q||p) holds for any joint distribution
over observed and unobserved variables, with no assumption about what form that
distribution takes. Everything downstream, the bound property and the monotonic increase,
follows from that one identity alone.
</Recall>

<Recall question="How does the E-step's optimal q connect back to the Q function from 9.3?" concepts={["complete-data-log-likelihood"]}>
Substituting the E-step's optimal q into L(q,theta) leaves exactly Q(theta,theta_old) plus a
term that does not depend on theta, so maximising L over theta at that q is the same
optimisation as maximising Q, tying the general argument back to the concrete update rule.
</Recall>
