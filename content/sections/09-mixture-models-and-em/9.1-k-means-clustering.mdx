## Intuition

Sometimes nobody hands you labels. You have a pile of points and a hunch that they fall
into groups, and the task is to find the groups from the points alone. K-means is the
plainest way to try: guess a handful of representative locations, hand every point to
whichever representative it is closest to, then move each representative to sit in the
middle of the points now claiming it. Repeat.

Neither move can make things worse, which is why the procedure is trustworthy enough to
reach for first. It is also why it is not the whole story. Every point must fully commit to
one group the moment it is closer to that group's centre, even by a hair, and a point sitting
exactly between two groups gets no say in the matter. That all-or-nothing commitment is the
one thing the rest of this chapter exists to soften.

## The math

Write $r_{nk}=1$ if point $n$ is currently assigned to cluster $k$, else $0$. The quantity
being minimised is the total squared distance from every point to its own cluster's centre:

<MathBlock id="9.1" name="distortion measure">

$$J = \sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{K} r_{nk} \lVert \mathbf{x}_n - \boldsymbol{\mu}_k \rVert^2$$

</MathBlock>

$J$ is linear in $\{r_{nk}\}$ for fixed $\{\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\}$, and quadratic in
$\{\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\}$ for fixed $\{r_{nk}\}$, so minimising each in turn is closed-form
both times. Fixing the means, the best assignment sends each point to its nearest one:

<MathBlock id="9.2" name="hard assignment">

$$r_{nk} = \begin{cases} 1 & k = \arg\min_j \lVert \mathbf{x}_n - \boldsymbol{\mu}_j \rVert^2 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

</MathBlock>

Fixing the assignment, the best mean is the average of its members:

<MathBlock id="9.4" name="mean update">

$$\boldsymbol{\mu}_k = \frac{\sum_n r_{nk}\,\mathbf{x}_n}{\sum_n r_{nk}}$$

</MathBlock>

Each phase can only lower $J$ or leave it unchanged, and there are only finitely many ways
to partition $N$ points into $K$ groups, so the alternation must stop. It stops at *a*
minimum, not necessarily *the* minimum.

## Worked example

Seven points, two clusters, means started at $(0,0)$ and $(5,5)$: after one E-step and
M-step, the means move to $(0.875, 0.875)$ and $(5.33, 5.33)$, and $J$ falls from $16.5$ to
$9.708$. A second round changes nothing, so it has converged.

<Figure caption="The worked example: seven points, their converged assignment, the starting means (rings) and the converged means (crosses).">
  <WorkedExampleTrace client:visible />
</Figure>

Treat each pixel of an image as a point in colour space and K-means gives lossy
compression: store $K$ colours plus one label per pixel instead of a full colour per pixel.
For an $80\times60$ image at 24 bits per pixel, $K=2$ colours cost only $4.2\%$ of the raw
size; $K=8$ costs $12.7\%$. More colours, better fidelity, worse ratio.

## Recall

<Recall question="Why does K-means always terminate?" concepts={["distortion-measure"]}>
Each phase minimises $J$ exactly given the other set of variables fixed, so $J$ never
increases. There are only finitely many ways to partition $N$ points into $K$ groups, so a
non-increasing sequence over a finite set of values must eventually repeat, at which point
neither phase changes anything.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Does K-means terminating mean it found the best clustering?" concepts={["k-means-clustering"]}>
No. It guarantees a local minimum of $J$, not the global one. Different random starting
means can converge to different final partitions with different final distortions, which is
why running it from several starts and keeping the best is standard practice.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What single commitment does the assignment step (9.2) force on every point?" concepts={["k-means-clustering"]}>
Every point belongs entirely to exactly one cluster, decided solely by which mean is
nearest, even for a point almost exactly between two means. There is no notion of a point
partly belonging to two clusters.
</Recall>

<Recall question="How does K-means turn into lossy image compression?" concepts={["vector-quantization"]}>
Cluster the pixels' colours, then replace every pixel by its cluster's mean colour. Storage
only needs the $K$ mean colours plus one label per pixel, rather than a full colour per
pixel, at the cost of every pixel in a cluster now sharing one approximate colour.
</Recall>
