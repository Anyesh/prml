## Intuition

Metropolis-Hastings needs a proposal distribution tuned by hand, and getting that tuning
wrong is either slow mixing or high rejection. Gibbs sampling sidesteps the choice
entirely: at each step, replace one variable with a fresh draw from its own exact
conditional distribution given every other variable's current value.

That conditional draw is never rejected, because it already is a draw from the right
distribution for that one coordinate. The catch shows up when the variables are correlated:
moving one coordinate at a time along a diagonal ridge makes almost no progress along the
ridge itself, no matter how cheap each individual step is.

## The math

Cycling through three variables in turn, the first update draws

<MathBlock id="11.46" name="Gibbs conditional update">

$$p(z_1 \mid z_2^{(\tau)}, z_3^{(\tau)})$$

</MathBlock>

and so on for $z_2$, then $z_3$, each conditioned on whatever the others currently hold.
Treated as a Metropolis-Hastings step that only touches $z_k$, the acceptance probability
collapses to exactly one:

<MathBlock id="11.49" name="Gibbs acceptance probability is always one">

$$A(z^\star, z) = \frac{\tilde{p}(z^\star)}{\tilde{p}(z)} \cdot \frac{q_k(z \mid z^\star)}{q_k(z^\star \mid z)} = \frac{p(z_k^\star \mid z_{\backslash k})}{p(z_k \mid z_{\backslash k})} \cdot \frac{p(z_k \mid z_{\backslash k})}{p(z_k^\star \mid z_{\backslash k})} = 1$$

</MathBlock>

For a correlated Gaussian conditional of width $l$ inside a marginal of width $L$, the
random walk needs on the order of $(L/l)^2$ sweeps to reach an independent state:

<Figure caption="Steps to independence explode only near the extremes of correlation." width="wide">
  <StepsToIndependence client:visible />
</Figure>

Over-relaxation counters this by biasing the draw away from the current value instead of
centring it there:

<MathBlock id="11.50" name="over-relaxation update">

$$z_i' = \mu_i + \alpha(z_i - \mu_i) + \sigma_i(1 - \alpha^2)^{1/2}\nu$$

</MathBlock>

for $-1 < \alpha < 1$ and $\nu$ a standard normal draw; $\alpha = 0$ is plain Gibbs.

## Worked example

<Figure caption="Same correlated target, same start: over-relaxation covers the distance to equilibrium in far fewer sweeps." width="wide">
  <OverRelaxationComparison client:visible />
</Figure>

At correlation 0.95, plain Gibbs starting at $(4,-4)$ needs dozens of sweeps to reach the
origin's neighbourhood because each conditional step has variance $1 - 0.95^2 = 0.0975$.
Setting $\alpha = -0.8$ in (11.50) biases every draw to the far side of its conditional
mean, which is why its distance curve above drops faster without changing what either
conditional distribution actually is.

## Recall

<Recall question="Why is a Gibbs sampling step always accepted?" concepts={["gibbs-sampling"]}>
Seen as a Metropolis-Hastings step touching one variable, the proposal is exactly the true
conditional for that variable, which makes the acceptance ratio (11.49) equal to one by
construction.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What determines how many sweeps Gibbs sampling needs for an independent sample from a correlated Gaussian?" concepts={["gibbs-sampling"]}>
Roughly $(L/l)^2$, the squared ratio of the marginal width to the conditional width: the
narrower the conditional relative to the marginal, the slower the ridge is crossed.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What does over-relaxation change, and what does it leave alone?" concepts={["over-relaxation"]}>
It biases each draw towards the opposite side of the conditional mean when alpha is
negative, encouraging directed motion; it leaves that conditional's own mean and variance
unchanged; either is a distribution the coordinate could equally have been drawn from.
</Recall>
