## Intuition

Metropolis-Hastings hands the step-size problem to whoever tunes it: too small and the
chain crawls, too large and almost nothing is accepted. Slice sampling instead lets the
target itself set the scale at every single step, growing or shrinking a window until it
roughly matches how wide the distribution is right where the chain currently sits.

The trick is to stop thinking of sampling as choosing a step size at all, and instead
imagine sampling a point uniformly from the two-dimensional area under the target's curve.
Do that, throw away the vertical coordinate, and what remains is a sample from the target.
Nobody can draw from that whole area directly, but a small local search around the current
point can.

## The math

Augment $z$ with $u$ and sample uniformly under the curve:

<MathBlock id="11.51" name="augmented uniform distribution over (z, u)">

$$p(z, u) = \begin{cases} 1/Z_p & 0 \leqslant u \leqslant \tilde{p}(z) \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

</MathBlock>

Integrating out $u$ recovers exactly the target, with no leftover normalizing constant to
track:

<MathBlock id="11.52" name="marginal over z recovers p(z)">

$$\int p(z, u)\, \mathrm{d}u = \frac{\tilde{p}(z)}{Z_p} = p(z)$$

</MathBlock>

Sampling alternates: given $z$, draw $u$ uniform on $[0, \tilde{p}(z)]$; given $u$, draw $z$
uniform from the slice $\{z : \tilde{p}(z) > u\}$. That second draw is the part with no
closed form, which is why a window is grown outward until both ends fall outside the slice,
then shrunk inward around every rejected candidate until one lands inside it.

A narrow starting window mostly grows; a wide one mostly shrinks. Either way it ends up
matched to the local scale, not to whatever a person guessed in advance.

## Worked example

<Figure caption="One fixed Metropolis step size handles a narrow target well and a wide one badly; slice sampling handles both from the same starting window." width="wide">
  <SliceVsMetropolisStepSize client:visible />
</Figure>

Tuned to a target of standard deviation 0.30, a Metropolis step of 0.35 gives an
autocorrelation time near 6.5. Point the same step size at a target five times wider and
that time jumps past 55: nothing about the algorithm changed except the problem. Slice
sampling, started with the same window on both problems, stays near 1.4 either way.

## Recall

<Recall question="What does slice sampling do with the vertical variable u after using it?" concepts={["slice-sampling"]}>
Nothing; it is discarded. Its only job was defining the horizontal slice the next z is
drawn from (11.51-11.52).
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why does slice sampling need both a growing step and a shrinking step?" concepts={["slice-sampling"]}>
The initial window might not cover the whole slice, so it grows outward until both ends
fall outside; a candidate drawn from it might still land outside the slice, so failed draws
shrink the window inward instead of being discarded outright.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What problem does slice sampling solve that a fixed-variance Metropolis proposal cannot?" concepts={["slice-sampling"]}>
It removes the need to hand-tune a single step size: the window's width adapts on its own
to whatever scale the target happens to have at the current point.
</Recall>
