## Intuition

Rejection and importance sampling both draw every candidate from scratch, blind to
everything drawn before it. That blindness is exactly what makes them collapse in high
dimensions: a proposal with no memory has to cover the whole space at once.

A Markov chain fixes this by remembering where it is. Each new candidate is proposed near
the current state rather than out of nowhere, so it only has to look plausible locally, not
globally. The price is that consecutive samples are now correlated, sometimes badly, and the
chain has to be run long enough for that correlation to fade before it is trustworthy.

That square-root crawl is the cost every method in this chapter is really fighting.

## The math

A first-order Markov chain only remembers one step back:

<MathBlock id="11.37" name="first-order Markov property">

$$p(z^{(m+1)} \mid z^{(1)}, \ldots, z^{(m)}) = p(z^{(m+1)} \mid z^{(m)})$$

</MathBlock>

A distribution is invariant under the chain's transitions $T$ if running one step leaves it
unchanged:

<MathBlock id="11.39" name="invariant distribution">

$$p^\star(z) = \sum_{z'} T(z', z)\, p^\star(z')$$

</MathBlock>

Detailed balance is the easy sufficient condition for this: probability flowing $z \to z'$
must exactly match probability flowing back.

<MathBlock id="11.40" name="detailed balance">

$$p^\star(z)\, T(z, z') = p^\star(z')\, T(z', z)$$

</MathBlock>

The basic Metropolis algorithm proposes from a symmetric $q$ and accepts with

<MathBlock id="11.33" name="basic Metropolis acceptance probability">

$$A(z^\star, z^{(\tau)}) = \min\left(1, \frac{\tilde{p}(z^\star)}{\tilde{p}(z^{(\tau)})}\right)$$

</MathBlock>

Metropolis-Hastings drops the symmetry requirement by correcting for it directly:

<MathBlock id="11.44" name="Metropolis-Hastings acceptance probability">

$$A_k(z^\star, z^{(\tau)}) = \min\left(1, \frac{\tilde{p}(z^\star)\, q_k(z^{(\tau)} \mid z^\star)}{\tilde{p}(z^{(\tau)})\, q_k(z^\star \mid z^{(\tau)})}\right)$$

</MathBlock>

## Recall

<Recall question="Why does a proposal that depends on the current state avoid the problem that sank rejection sampling?" concepts={["markov-chain-monte-carlo"]}>
It only has to look plausible locally, near where the chain already is, instead of matching
the whole target distribution's shape everywhere at once.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What does detailed balance guarantee, and what does it not guarantee?" concepts={["detailed-balance"]}>
It guarantees the target distribution is invariant under the chain (11.40-11.41). It does
not by itself guarantee ergodicity, so a chain can satisfy it and still fail to converge.
</Recall>

<Recall question="When does the Metropolis-Hastings acceptance rule reduce to the plain Metropolis rule?" concepts={["metropolis-hastings"]}>
Exactly when the proposal is symmetric, $q(z_A\mid z_B) = q(z_B\mid z_A)$, which cancels the
proposal ratio out of (11.44) and leaves (11.33).
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why is a tiny proposal step size bad even though it accepts almost everything?" concepts={["metropolis-hastings"]}>
High acceptance with tiny steps still moves through state space as a slow random walk, so
consecutive samples stay correlated for a long time.
</Recall>
