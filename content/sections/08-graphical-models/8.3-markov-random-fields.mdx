## Intuition

An arrow encodes something an undirected edge cannot: which variable is the cause. Some
relationships have no natural direction at all, neighbouring pixels in an image, say,
where each constrains the other symmetrically rather than one generating the other.
Undirected graphs are built for exactly that case.

The price is the collider's asymmetry. Every local independence rule becomes the single
plain statement it always secretly was: a node is independent of the rest of the graph
given its immediate neighbours, full stop, no reversal waiting to happen under
conditioning.

What replaces directed factorization is a product over cliques, small fully-connected
clusters of nodes, of an arbitrary positive function each. Nothing forces those functions
to be probabilities of anything, so the whole product needs to be renormalised at the
end. That single global normaliser is the one number a directed graph never had to
compute and an undirected one always does.

## The math

A node is independent of every other node given its neighbours alone:

<MathBlock id="8.37" name="undirected local independence">

$$p(x_i \mid x_{\mathcal{V}\setminus i}) = p(x_i \mid x_{\mathcal{N}_i})$$

</MathBlock>

<Figure caption="A lattice: every edge is a clique of size two, and every node's Markov blanket is just its drawn neighbours." source="8.30">
  <MRFLatticeDiagram client:visible />
</Figure>

The joint factorizes over cliques $C$, each with its own potential $\psi_C \geq 0$:

<MathBlock id="8.38" name="clique factorization">

$$p(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{Z} \prod_{C} \psi_C(\mathbf{x}_C)$$

</MathBlock>

<MathBlock id="8.39" name="partition function">

$$Z = \sum_{\mathbf{x}} \prod_{C} \psi_C(\mathbf{x}_C)$$

</MathBlock>

Writing potentials as exponentials keeps them positive automatically and turns the
product into a sum of energies:

<MathBlock id="8.41" name="Boltzmann energy form">

$$\psi_C(\mathbf{x}_C) = \exp\big(-E(\mathbf{x}_C)\big)$$

</MathBlock>

For binary image de-noising, latent pixels $x_i$ and noisy observed pixels $y_i$ in
$\{-1,+1\}$, coupling neighbours together and each pixel to its own observation:

<MathBlock id="8.42" name="Ising de-noising energy">

$$E(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = h \sum_i x_i - \beta \sum_{\{i,j\}} x_i x_j - \eta \sum_i x_i y_i$$

</MathBlock>

<Figure caption="One pixel's total energy with everything else fixed, at -1 versus +1. Four agreeing neighbours and a matching observation make +1 far cheaper.">
  <LocalEnergyBars client:visible />
</Figure>

<Figure caption="Final energy after five ICM sweeps as coupling strength beta rises, on a fixed noisy pattern. Energy falls, then the curve flattens once smoothing has nothing more to gain." width="wide">
  <EnergyVsCouplingCurve client:visible />
</Figure>

Every directed graph can be converted to this form by moralizing: connect every pair of
co-parents, then drop all arrowheads.

<MathBlock id="8.49" name="moralization construction">

$$\psi_{\mathrm{pa}_k \cup \{k\}}(\mathbf{x}) = p(x_k \mid \mathrm{pa}_k)$$

</MathBlock>

<Figure caption="A collider moralized: the missing edge between its two causes is added before the arrows are dropped, so the undirected version keeps exactly the same independence structure.">
  <MoralizationDiagram client:visible />
</Figure>

Iterated conditional modes flips one pixel at a time to whichever state lowers 8.42,
holding every other pixel fixed. It only ever decreases the energy, so it always settles,
just not always on the cleanest image.

## Worked example

A 20x20 plus-sign image, 8% of pixels flipped, $\eta=2.1$, $h=0$. At $\beta=1.0$, one
sweep already drops the mismatch against the noisy input from 32 flipped pixels to a
handful, and the energy computed from 8.42 falls every single sweep, never once rising,
exactly as ICM's coordinate-descent guarantee requires.

Pushed to $\beta=3.0$ on the same image, the energy after five sweeps is lower still, but
the extra drop comes from flattening genuine edges of the plus sign, not from removing
more noise: the neighbour term now dominates the data term enough that agreeing with a
neighbour outweighs agreeing with the actual observation.

## Recall

<Recall question="What single fact replaces every directed independence rule in an undirected graph?" concepts={["markov-random-field"]}>
A node is independent of the rest of the graph given its neighbours, with no collider
exception. Undirected graphs have no head-to-head nodes to reverse the rule.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why must clique potentials be exponentiated into an energy rather than left as arbitrary positive numbers?" concepts={["clique-potential"]}>
They do not have to be, but writing them as exp(-E) guarantees positivity for free and
turns the product in 8.38 into a sum, which is far easier to optimise and to reason about
term by term.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why does ICM always terminate?" concepts={["iterated-conditional-modes"]}>
Each flip is chosen to not increase 8.42, and the energy is bounded below by the finite
sum over a finite image, so a non-increasing bounded sequence must stop moving.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why does moralizing a directed graph add edges between co-parents specifically?" concepts={["moralization"]}>
Because a conditional p(x_k | parents) genuinely couples all of those parents together
inside one clique; dropping the arrows without connecting them would silently claim an
independence the directed graph never asserted.
</Recall>
