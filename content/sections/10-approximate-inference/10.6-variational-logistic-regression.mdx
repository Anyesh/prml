## Intuition

Chapter 4's Laplace approximation handles Bayesian logistic regression by fitting a single
Gaussian to the mode of the exact posterior, information from everywhere else in that
posterior discarded once the curvature at the peak is read off. It works, but it is a
one-shot summary, not a fit that could recognisably be pushed further.

Bounding every observation's likelihood with the previous section's local sigmoid bound
gives a full alternative: multiply a Gaussian prior by likelihood terms that are each
already Gaussian-shaped in the weights, and the posterior comes out Gaussian exactly,
not approximately at a point. The price is one extra number per observation, the
touch-point of its own bound, fit alongside the weights rather than chosen in advance.

Nothing about this needs the data seen all at once. Each observation contributes through
its own bound parameter, so the same machine that fits ten points fits ten thousand,
one local tangent per row.

## The math

Bounding $p(t_n \mid \mathbf{w})$ with 10.144 turns the log joint into something quadratic
in $\mathbf{w}$, and the mean-field factor $q(\mathbf{w})$ comes out Gaussian with
parameters built from every observation's own $\xi_n$:

<MathBlock id="10.157" name="posterior mean">

$$\mathbf{m}_N = \mathbf{S}_N \Big[\mathbf{S}_0^{-1}\mathbf{m}_0 + \sum_n (t_n - \tfrac12)\boldsymbol{\phi}_n\Big]$$

</MathBlock>

<MathBlock id="10.158" name="posterior precision">

$$\mathbf{S}_N^{-1} = \mathbf{S}_0^{-1} + 2\sum_n \lambda(\xi_n)\,\boldsymbol{\phi}_n\boldsymbol{\phi}_n^{\mathrm{T}}$$

</MathBlock>

Each $\xi_n$ is then re-fit to the second moment of $\boldsymbol{\phi}_n^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{w}$
under the current $q(\mathbf{w})$:

<MathBlock id="10.163" name="xi re-estimation">

$$(\xi_n^{\text{new}})^2 = \boldsymbol{\phi}_n^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{S}_N + \mathbf{m}_N\mathbf{m}_N^{\mathrm{T}})\boldsymbol{\phi}_n$$

</MathBlock>

<Figure caption="Every observation's xi_n across coordinate-ascent rounds. Points near the boundary settle at a larger value than points the model is already confident about.">
  <XiConvergence client:visible />
</Figure>

Cycling 10.157-10.163 to a fixed point maximises the bound below, in closed form because
every term involved is Gaussian or a Gaussian moment:

<MathBlock id="10.164" name="closed-form lower bound">

$$\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\xi}) = \tfrac12\ln\frac{|\mathbf{S}_N|}{|\mathbf{S}_0|} - \tfrac12\mathbf{m}_N^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{S}_N^{-1}\mathbf{m}_N + \tfrac12\mathbf{m}_0^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{S}_0^{-1}\mathbf{m}_0 + \sum_n\Big[\ln\sigma(\xi_n) - \tfrac{\xi_n}{2} - \lambda(\xi_n)\xi_n^2\Big]$$

</MathBlock>

Giving the prior precision its own Gamma posterior extends this exactly as section 10.3
extended plain Bayesian regression:

<MathBlock id="10.178" name="alpha shape update">

$$a_N = a_0 + \frac{M}{2}$$

</MathBlock>

<MathBlock id="10.182" name="expected alpha">

$$\mathbb{E}[\alpha] = \frac{a_N}{b_N}$$

</MathBlock>

## Worked example

Two well-separated clusters of fourteen points each, fit with a broad isotropic prior. The
mean boundary lands almost exactly midway between the class centres, unsurprising for
balanced, separable data. What 10.158 adds is visible only in the sampled boundaries: near
the origin, where the two classes' spread overlaps slightly, boundaries drawn from
$q(\mathbf{w})$ fan out by several degrees of slope, while far from the origin every
sampled boundary tracks the mean to within a hair, because $\mathbf{S}_N$ shrinks fastest
in directions the data pins down hardest.

## Recall

<Recall question="What does bounding the sigmoid buy that the Laplace approximation does not?" concepts={["variational-logistic-regression"]}>
A Gaussian posterior reached by explicitly optimising a lower bound on the true evidence,
rather than a Gaussian read off the curvature at one point of the true posterior with no
bound guaranteeing how good that summary is.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why does each observation need its own xi_n rather than one shared value?" concepts={["variational-logistic-regression"]}>
The local bound of 10.5 is tight at one point per curve; a single xi could not sit close to
every observation's own $\phi_n^T w$ at once, so 10.163 lets each observation choose its
own touch point.
</Recall>

<Recall question="How does hyperparameter inference here extend section 10.3?" concepts={["variational-logistic-regression"]}>
Identically: the prior precision alpha gets its own Gamma posterior (10.178) instead of a
fixed value, fit by the same outer coordinate ascent, now wrapped around a Gaussian
posterior built from bounded likelihood terms instead of an exact Gaussian likelihood.
</Recall>
