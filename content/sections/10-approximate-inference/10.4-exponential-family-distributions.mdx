## Intuition

Every worked example so far has needed its own derivation: a Gaussian's mean and
precision update one way, a mixture's responsibilities another, a regression's weight
precision a third. Underneath, all three did the same thing, because a Gaussian, a
Dirichlet, a Wishart, and a Gamma are all members of one family with a shared shape.

Restricting a model to conditional distributions built from that shared shape turns the
general mean-field update into something that no longer needs re-deriving per model.
Every node's optimal factor depends only on the nodes touching it, and updating it is a
fixed, generic computation on messages passed along the edges of the graph. Naming this
once buys every future conjugate model the update for free.

That locality is the useful part, not a footnote. A model with a thousand nodes never
needs its own thousand-term derivation; it needs the one local rule, applied wherever an
edge exists.

## The math

A model built entirely from exponential-family conditionals writes its complete-data
likelihood as

<MathBlock id="10.113" name="complete-data exponential family">

$$p(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Z} \mid \boldsymbol{\eta}) = \prod_{n} h(\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{z}_n)\, g(\boldsymbol{\eta})\, \exp\big(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{z}_n)\big)$$

</MathBlock>

Applying the general mean-field rule to one node gives a factor that only reads its
neighbours:

<MathBlock id="10.115" name="optimal factor for one node">

$$\ln q^{*}(\mathbf{Z}) = \sum_n \Big[\ln h(\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{z}_n) + \mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\mathrm{T}}]\, \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{z}_n)\Big] + \text{const}$$

</MathBlock>

<Figure caption="The Markov blanket of one latent node z: parents, children, and children's other parents. Nothing further out ever enters its update." source="10.5">
  <MarkovBlanketHighlight client:visible />
</Figure>

Conjugacy makes the parameter node's own posterior stay in the prior's family too, with
its natural parameter simply accumulating a contribution per observation:

<MathBlock id="10.119" name="natural-parameter posterior">

$$q^{*}(\boldsymbol{\eta}) = f(\nu_N, \boldsymbol{\chi}_N)\, g(\boldsymbol{\eta})^{\nu_N} \exp(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\mathrm{T}}\boldsymbol{\chi}_N)$$

</MathBlock>

<MathBlock id="10.121" name="natural-parameter update">

$$\boldsymbol{\chi}_N = \boldsymbol{\chi}_0 + \sum_n \mathbb{E}_{z_n}[\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{z}_n)]$$

</MathBlock>

</Figure>

## Worked example

The variational mixture's own responsibility update is 10.115 read directly off its
graph: $z_n$'s Markov blanket is $\{\boldsymbol{\pi}, \mathbf{x}_n, \boldsymbol{\mu},
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\}$, and its optimal factor needs exactly $\mathbb{E}[\ln\pi_k]$,
$\mathbb{E}[\ln|\Lambda_k|]$, and $\mathbb{E}_{\mu_k,\Lambda_k}[\cdots]$: three
expectations from three neighbours, nothing from further away.

The Dirichlet parameter $\alpha_k$ is 10.121 in its simplest form: it starts at $\alpha_0$
and adds exactly one responsibility per point, so after $N$ points it reads
$\alpha_0 + \sum_n r_{nk}$, the same $N_k$-based update seen twice already, now visibly a
running sum rather than a batch formula.

## Recall

<Recall question="Why does exponential-family structure make the mean-field update local?" concepts={["variational-message-passing"]}>
10.115 shows the optimal factor for any node depends only on the expected sufficient
statistics of its neighbours in the graph, never on nodes further away, because the log
joint factorises node by node.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What does a conjugate node's natural parameter do as data arrives?" concepts={["variational-message-passing"]}>
It accumulates: 10.121 adds one neighbour's expected sufficient statistic per observation
to the prior's own natural parameter, exactly a running sum rather than a batch refit.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why is one general update rule enough for every conjugate-exponential model?" concepts={["mean-field-approximation"]}>
Because 10.115 is derived once from the exponential family's shared form, not per
distribution; any model built from such conditionals inherits the same local computation
without a fresh derivation.
</Recall>
