## Intuition

EM for a Gaussian mixture always uses every component it is handed. Offer it six clusters
for data that only has three, and it will split the extra three across the data anyway,
because nothing in the maximum-likelihood objective rewards leaving one empty.

Putting a prior on the mixing weights changes that. A component with no data pulling on it
has nothing to override the prior's own preference for a small share, and a Dirichlet prior
concentrated near zero prefers exactly that: most of the weight, most of the time, on as
few components as it can get away with. The result is a fit that can delete a component on
its own, purely by the same coordinate ascent chapter 9 already used, now applied one level
up.

## The math

Responsibilities generalise 9.13 with the parameters replaced by their expectations under
the current Gaussian-Wishart and Dirichlet posteriors:

<MathBlock id="10.46" name="log responsibility">

$$\ln \rho_{nk} = \mathbb{E}[\ln \pi_k] + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\ln|\Lambda_k|] - \tfrac{D}{2}\ln 2\pi - \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_k,\Lambda_k}\big[(\mathbf{x}_n-\boldsymbol\mu_k)^{\mathrm{T}}\Lambda_k(\mathbf{x}_n-\boldsymbol\mu_k)\big]$$

</MathBlock>

<MathBlock id="10.49" name="responsibility normalisation">

$$r_{nk} = \frac{\rho_{nk}}{\sum_j \rho_{nj}}$$

</MathBlock>

</Figure>

The M step updates the Dirichlet and Gaussian-Wishart posteriors from the same weighted
statistics chapter 9 computed, $N_k$, $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_k$, $\mathbf{S}_k$:

<MathBlock id="10.58" name="Dirichlet update">

$$\alpha_k = \alpha_0 + N_k$$

</MathBlock>

<MathBlock id="10.63" name="Wishart degrees-of-freedom update">

$$\nu_k = \nu_0 + N_k$$

</MathBlock>

A component nothing is responsible for has $N_k \approx 0$, so 10.58 and 10.63 (and every
other M-step update) simply return the prior. No special case deletes a component; the
same update that grows a useful one starves an unused one automatically.

<Figure caption="Converged lower bound against the number of components offered, on data with three true clusters. It peaks exactly at three." source="10.7">
  <LowerBoundVsK client:visible />
</Figure>

The lower bound itself sums seven expectation terms and is what a model-comparison figure
like the one above actually maximises. The predictive density marginalises the
posterior in closed form to a mixture of Student-t distributions:

<MathBlock id="10.81" name="predictive Student-t mixture">

$$p(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{X}) = \frac{1}{\hat\alpha}\sum_k \alpha_k\, \mathrm{St}(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{m}_k, \mathbf{L}_k, \nu_k+1-D)$$

</MathBlock>

not a Gaussian mixture: uncertainty in the precision itself thickens each component's tails.

## Worked example

With three true clusters and six offered components, the fit above starts every mixing
weight near $1/6$. By the fifth sweep, the three components nearest an empty region already
have weight below $0.02$; by the last sweep the survivors sum to over $0.97$ of the total,
each sitting close to one true cluster. EM given the same six starts and the same data
never drops below roughly $1/6$ on any component, because its objective has no term that
penalises using one.

## Recall

<Recall question="Why does a Gaussian-Wishart prior keep every posterior factor in closed form across iterations?" concepts={["gaussian-wishart-prior"]}>
It is conjugate to the Gaussian likelihood for an unknown mean and precision taken
together, so multiplying it by the responsibility-weighted likelihood in the M step returns
another Gaussian-Wishart. The family never has to change across sweeps.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What mechanism actually prunes a surplus component?" concepts={["automatic-component-pruning"]}>
Nothing special-cased. A component with near-zero effective count N_k has every one of its
M-step updates (10.58, 10.63, and the rest) revert to the prior, because the data term in
each update is scaled by N_k.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why is the predictive density a Student-t mixture rather than a Gaussian one?" concepts={["responsibility"]}>
Because the predictive marginalises out the posterior's own uncertainty about each
component's precision, not just its mean. Averaging a Gaussian over an unknown precision
with a Gamma-family distribution is exactly the derivation behind the Student-t.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Does raising the offered component count K risk over-fitting the way it would in EM?" concepts={["automatic-component-pruning"]}>
Not the same way. The lower bound in the figure above peaks at the true K and does not keep
rising past it, because surplus components are pruned rather than fit to noise.
</Recall>
