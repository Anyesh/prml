## Intuition

Mean-field always minimises the same direction of KL divergence, and the first section of
this chapter showed exactly what that costs: a compact answer that locks onto part of the
truth and reports too little uncertainty about the rest. Sometimes that cost is too high,
and the other direction, the one that spreads out to cover everything, is worth reaching
for instead.

The catch is that the other direction has no general closed-form recipe the way mean-field
does. Expectation propagation gets there anyway, by working locally rather than globally:
refine one piece of the approximation at a time, matching it to the true local behaviour
by moments rather than by an intractable global integral, and repeat until nothing moves.

Each refinement briefly forgets what it already believed, asks what the true model alone
would say, and folds the answer back in. That act of temporarily forgetting is the whole
trick, and it is the one operation mean-field never performs.

## The math

The approximate posterior stays a product of simple site factors throughout:

<MathBlock id="10.191" name="fully factorised EP posterior">

$$q(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \frac{1}{Z} \prod_i \tilde{f}_i(\boldsymbol{\theta})$$

</MathBlock>

Refining one site removes it to form a cavity, then re-admits the true factor in its
place and asks what Gaussian matches the result's first two moments:

<MathBlock id="10.197" name="tilted-distribution normaliser">

$$Z_j = \int f_j(\boldsymbol{\theta})\, q^{\backslash j}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\, d\boldsymbol{\theta}$$

</MathBlock>

<Figure caption="The cavity, the true (non-Gaussian) tilted density formed by multiplying in the real likelihood factor, and the Gaussian that matches its mean and variance exactly.">
  <MomentMatchingIllustration client:visible />
</Figure>

The refined site is read off from the ratio of the matched result to the cavity, which is
never itself required to be a proper density:

<MathBlock id="10.207" name="refined site factor">

$$\tilde{f}_j(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = Z_j \frac{q^{\text{new}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{q^{\backslash j}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}$$

</MathBlock>

For a signal buried in background clutter, the true factor is a two-component mixture the
cavity alone cannot represent:

<MathBlock id="10.209" name="clutter likelihood">

$$p(x_n \mid \theta) = (1-w)\,\mathcal{N}(x_n \mid \theta, 1) + w\,\mathcal{N}(x_n \mid 0, a)$$

</MathBlock>

<Figure caption="10.209's two components at theta = 2.5: a narrow signal peak sitting on a broad clutter background, and their sum.">
  <ClutterLikelihoodMixture client:visible />
</Figure>

and moment matching against it produces, as a byproduct, a genuine posterior probability
that a given point was signal rather than noise:

<MathBlock id="10.219" name="posterior signal probability">

$$\rho_n = 1 - \frac{w}{Z_n}\mathcal{N}(x_n \mid 0, a)$$

</MathBlock>

## Worked example

Eight points, five drawn near $\theta=2.5$ and three drawn as wide background clutter, fed
through one EP sweep against a broad prior. The exact posterior, available here only
because the problem is small enough to normalise on a grid, and EP's Gaussian land on
almost the same mean; the difference worth noticing is in the spread.

## Recall

<Recall question="Which KL direction does expectation propagation minimise, and where?" concepts={["kl-divergence-asymmetry"]}>
The reverse, mass-covering direction, but only locally: each site refinement moment-matches
against one true factor at a time, never against the full joint the way an exact reverse-KL
fit would need to.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What does removing a site to form a cavity accomplish?" concepts={["expectation-propagation"]}>
It stops a factor from being refined against information it already contributed, so the
moment match sees only the cavity's belief before this factor and the true likelihood, the
same principle a leave-one-out estimate uses elsewhere.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why does the clutter problem need moment matching rather than an exact update?" concepts={["ep-moment-matching"]}>
The true factor is a two-component mixture, and the product of a Gaussian cavity with it is
not Gaussian. Matching only the first two moments is what forces the result back into a
family EP can keep multiplying together.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What does a low posterior signal probability for one point indicate?" concepts={["ep-moment-matching"]}>
By 10.219, that the clutter component of 10.209 explains that observation better than the
signal component does, given everything else EP currently believes about theta.
</Recall>
