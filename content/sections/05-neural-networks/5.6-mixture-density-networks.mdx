## Intuition

Sum-of-squares error assumes one right answer per input, corrupted by symmetric noise. A
robot arm's inverse kinematics has two elbow positions that reach the same point; an
inverted sine has two x values behind the same t. Neither is noise. Both answers are
correct, and averaging them gives a point that is not.

That average is exactly what a conditional-mean network produces, because sum-of-squares
error is minimised by the mean of whatever the true distribution is, single-peaked or not.
Through the ambiguous region it draws a confident curve through empty space.

The fix keeps the network but changes what it outputs: not a single value but the
parameters of a small mixture of Gaussians, one distribution per plausible branch, with
weights that shift smoothly as the true answer moves from being one of several to being the
only one left.

## The math

The target is modelled as a mixture, parameters all functions of the input:

<MathBlock id="5.148" name="mixture model">

$$p(\mathbf{t}\mid\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k(\mathbf{x})\,\mathcal{N}\big(\mathbf{t}\mid \boldsymbol{\mu}_k(\mathbf{x}), \sigma_k^2(\mathbf{x})\big)$$

</MathBlock>

A softmax keeps the mixing coefficients a valid distribution over branches:

<MathBlock id="5.150" name="mixing coefficients">

$$\pi_k(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\exp(a_k^\pi)}{\sum_l \exp(a_l^\pi)}$$

</MathBlock>

<Figure caption="Mixing coefficients across the input range for a trained three-component network. Two components take turns dominating; the third stays a minor contributor almost everywhere." width="wide">
  <MixingCoefficientsAcrossInput client:visible />
</Figure>

Training minimises the negative log likelihood of the whole mixture at once:

<MathBlock id="5.153" name="mixture error function">

$$E(\mathbf{w}) = -\sum_n \ln\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k(\mathbf{x}_n,\mathbf{w})\,\mathcal{N}\big(\mathbf{t}_n\mid \boldsymbol{\mu}_k(\mathbf{x}_n,\mathbf{w}), \sigma_k^2(\mathbf{x}_n,\mathbf{w})\big)\right)$$

</MathBlock>

## Worked example

Collapsing the mixture to a single number the way 5.158 defines still has a use, provided
its spread is read alongside it rather than ignored:

<MathBlock id="5.158" name="predictive mean">

$$\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{t}\mid\mathbf{x}] = \sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k(\mathbf{x})\boldsymbol{\mu}_k(\mathbf{x})$$

</MathBlock>

<Figure caption="Total predictive variance across the same input range. It rises through the region where the two branches are both live, exactly where a single mean is least trustworthy." width="wide">
  <PredictiveVarianceAcrossInput client:visible />
</Figure>

The variance climbs right where the earlier density plot showed two ridges rather than one:
it is the mixture's own warning that its mean is a summary, not an answer.

## Recall

<Recall question="Why does a sum-of-squares network fail on a genuinely multi-valued target, rather than just fitting it noisily?" concepts={["mixture-density-network"]}>
Sum-of-squares error is minimised by the conditional mean, and when the true distribution
has separate branches, their mean sits between them, on a value neither branch produces.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What three quantities does a mixture density network output, and what keeps each one valid?" concepts={["mixture-density-network"]}>
Mixing coefficients through a softmax so they sum to one, standard deviations through an
exponential so they stay positive, and means output directly with no transformation needed.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What does a component's responsibility measure, here?" concepts={["responsibility", "mixture-density-network"]}>
How much of a given training target's likelihood that one component accounts for,
relative to the others; it is what each component's output gradient is weighted by.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why is the predictive mean alone not enough, even from a correctly fitted mixture?" concepts={["mixture-density-network"]}>
It collapses several branches back into one number exactly where a single number is least
informative; the predictive variance climbing in that same region is the model's own signal
not to trust the mean there.
</Recall>
