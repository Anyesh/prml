## Intuition

Section 12.1 finds a subspace by staring at one fixed dataset and asking which
directions carry the variance. That answer has no opinion about its own confidence, no
way to say whether a point is well explained or barely so, and no footing for comparing
two candidate subspace sizes against each other. It also cannot be run through the
general expectation-maximisation argument chapter 9 built, because nothing behind it is
an explicit probability model.

Probabilistic PCA supplies exactly that model: draw a low-dimensional latent point from
a standard Gaussian, map it linearly into the data space, and add isotropic noise. Fit
the loading matrix and noise level by maximum likelihood, and as the noise vanishes the
fit collapses onto the very subspace section 12.1 already found by a wholly different
argument. What is new is everything a generative model buys: a likelihood for comparing
dimensions, a route through missing coordinates, and an EM algorithm needing no full
covariance matrix at all.

## The math

A standard Gaussian latent variable, mapped linearly with isotropic noise:

<MathBlock id="12.31" name="latent prior">

$$p(\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$$

</MathBlock>

<MathBlock id="12.32" name="conditional data distribution">

$$p(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{W}\mathbf{z}+\boldsymbol{\mu},\sigma^2\mathbf{I})$$

</MathBlock>

Integrating $\mathbf{z}$ out leaves $\mathbf{x}$ itself Gaussian, with covariance

<MathBlock id="12.36" name="marginal covariance">

$$\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{T}} + \sigma^2\mathbf{I}$$

</MathBlock>

<Figure caption="Log-density contours of the fitted marginal over the data, with the latent axis drawn through the mean." width="wide">
  <MarginalDensityContour client:visible />
</Figure>

A rotation $\mathbf{W}\mathbf{R}$ by any orthogonal $\mathbf{R}$ leaves
$\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{T}}$, and so this whole marginal, unchanged: $\mathbf{W}$
is identifiable only up to rotation of the latent space.

<Figure caption="Two loading bases for the same data, related by a rotation R: slide it and watch the marginal ellipse never move." width="wide">
  <RotationalAmbiguity client:visible />
</Figure>

Maximum likelihood has a closed form (Tipping and Bishop, 1999b): with $\mathbf{U}_M$
the top $M$ eigenvectors of $\mathbf{S}$ and $\mathbf{L}_M$ their eigenvalues,

<MathBlock id="12.45" name="maximum-likelihood loading matrix">

$$\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{ML}} = \mathbf{U}_M(\mathbf{L}_M-\sigma^2\mathbf{I})^{1/2}$$

</MathBlock>

<MathBlock id="12.46" name="maximum-likelihood noise variance">

$$\sigma^2_{\mathrm{ML}} = \frac{1}{D-M}\sum_{i=M+1}^{D}\lambda_i$$

</MathBlock>

For large $D$, EM never forms $\mathbf{S}$ at all. The E step needs only

<MathBlock id="12.54" name="E-step latent mean">

$$\mathrm{E}[\mathbf{z}_n] = \mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{x}_n-\bar{\mathbf{x}})$$

</MathBlock>

and its second moment, and the M step refits both parameters from those alone:

<MathBlock id="12.56" name="M-step loading update">

$$\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{new}} = \Big[\sum_n(\mathbf{x}_n-\bar{\mathbf{x}})\mathrm{E}[\mathbf{z}_n]^{\mathrm{T}}\Big]\Big[\sum_n\mathrm{E}[\mathbf{z}_n\mathbf{z}_n^{\mathrm{T}}]\Big]^{-1}$$

</MathBlock>

<MathBlock id="12.57" name="M-step noise update">

$$\sigma^2_{\mathrm{new}} = \frac{1}{ND}\sum_n\Big\{\|\mathbf{x}_n-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|^2 - 2\mathrm{E}[\mathbf{z}_n]^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{new}}^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{x}_n-\bar{\mathbf{x}}) + \mathrm{Tr}\big(\mathrm{E}[\mathbf{z}_n\mathbf{z}_n^{\mathrm{T}}]\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{new}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{new}}\big)\Big\}$$

</MathBlock>

## Worked example

Sixty points generated along a hidden direction $(0.8, 0.6)$ with noise standard
deviation $0.4$ give, by the closed form, $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{ML}}=0.155$ and a loading
direction within about $4.1$ degrees of the true generator. Starting EM instead from a
near-isotropic guess ($\sigma^2=1$), the log-likelihood climbs from $-215.4$ to $-153.5$
over eight rounds, never once falling, with $\sigma^2$ down to $0.184$ and still
shrinking toward the closed-form value when the rounds run out.

<Figure caption="Sigma squared falling across EM rounds toward the closed-form maximum-likelihood value it never quite reaches in eight rounds.">
  <EmSigmaConvergence client:visible />
</Figure>

On data with one quiet axis and two noisy ones, factor analysis recovers noise
variances near $0.07$, $0.61$, and $0.53$, tracking the true asymmetry; PPCA's single
shared $\sigma^2=0.435$ cannot, and overstates the quiet axis's variance at $1.72$
against an empirical $1.62$.

<Figure caption="The quiet axis against a noisy one: PPCA's shared noise term misses what factor analysis's per-axis term catches." width="wide">

## Recall

<Recall question="What does probabilistic PCA have that ordinary PCA does not?" concepts={["probabilistic-pca"]}>
An explicit generative model with a likelihood, so dimensions can be compared, missing
data handled, and the fit run through EM. Its maximum-likelihood solution recovers
ordinary PCA exactly as the noise variance goes to zero.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why is the loading matrix W only identifiable up to rotation?" concepts={["rotational-ambiguity"]}>
The marginal covariance depends on W only through W W^T, and right-multiplying W by any
orthogonal matrix leaves that product, and so the whole model, unchanged.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What is the one modelling difference between factor analysis and PPCA?" concepts={["factor-analysis"]}>
PPCA's noise is isotropic, one shared sigma squared on every axis; factor analysis lets
each observed coordinate keep its own noise variance on the diagonal.
</Recall>

<Recall question="Why bother with EM when a closed form for PPCA already exists?" concepts={["expectation-maximisation"]}>
The closed form needs an eigendecomposition of the full covariance, costing order D
cubed. EM's E and M steps only ever touch M-dimensional quantities, which is far cheaper
once D is large and M is small.
</Recall>

<Recall question="What happens to the EM updates as sigma squared is driven to zero?" concepts={["probabilistic-pca"]}>
They reduce to ordinary PCA's power-iteration-like update: the posterior over z becomes
a deterministic projection, and the M step becomes exactly the reconstruction rule from
12.1.
</Recall>
